## 1. Context in Quick-Commerce Operations

Quick-commerce platforms (such as Blinkit, Zepto, and Swiggy Instamart) promise delivery times under 10–15 minutes. To sustain this service guarantee, dark stores operate with constrained physical footprint (typically 2,000–3,500 sq. ft.) and cannot afford excessive inventory accumulation or frequent out-of-stock events (OOS).

This module bridges the predictive demand models developed in **Review 1** with operational fulfillment strategies evaluated in **Review 2**.

## 2. Mathematical Formulations

### 2.1 Dynamic Safety Stock ($SS$) with Stochastic Lead Time

Traditional models assume fixed replenishment lead times. In urban quick-commerce logistics, replenishment lead times from Mother Hubs / Central Distribution Centers (CDCs) are subject to traffic and route congestion.

$$\sigma_{L,D} = \sqrt{L \cdot \sigma_D^2 + D^2 \cdot \sigma_L^2}$$

$$SS = Z \cdot \sigma_{L,D} = Z \cdot \sqrt{L \cdot \sigma_D^2 + D^2 \cdot \sigma_L^2}$$

Where:
- $Z$: Inverse cumulative distribution function of standard normal distribution corresponding to target Cycle Service Level ($CSL$).
- $L$: Expected replenishment lead time in days.
- $\sigma_L$: Standard deviation of replenishment lead time.
- $D$: Expected daily demand for SKU $i$ at dark store $s$.
- $\sigma_D$: Standard deviation of daily demand.

### 2.2 Continuous Review Reorder Point ($ROP$)

$$ROP = (D \cdot L) + SS$$

When inventory position drops to or below $ROP$, an automatic replenishment trigger is dispatched to the central hub.
